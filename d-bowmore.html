Seanchas · The story

The oldest on Islay

Bowmore was licensed in 1779, which makes it Islay's oldest distillery and one of the oldest in Scotland. The village around it was planned in 1768 by the laird, Daniel Campbell, as Islay's new capital.

Its No.1 Vaults, also from 1779, is partly below sea level, with the waters of Loch Indaal on the other side of the wall. Bowmore says it's the oldest whisky maturation warehouse in the world, and the casks pick up a salty edge from the sea air.

Bowmore malts about 30% of its own barley on the floor, peated to around 25 ppm, and buys the rest from Port Ellen Maltings. It runs four stills, takes its water from the River Laggan, and is owned by Suntory.

Don't miss: walk up Main Street to the Round Church at the top. It was built round in 1767, the story goes, so the Devil would have no corners to hide in. It's about five minutes from the distillery, and there's time for it and lunch before the 13:30 bus.

The distillery

Islay's oldest licensed distillery, on the shore of Loch Indaal in the island's capital. Its No.1 Vaults warehouse sits below sea level, and it still turns barley on its own malting floor.

House style. The middle path: gentle smoke, tropical fruit and honey, with a salty sea-breeze edge.

Bowmore is not distilling for the rest of 2026, but tours and tastings continue.

Ar turas · Our tour

Bowmore Signature Cask Experience

WhenTue 29 June, 10:30
Length1¼ hours
Price£25 each
In randabout R600

Bowmore's standard visit, and good value. A guide takes you through the choices that make Bowmore taste the way it does, then you taste in a traditional warehouse by Loch Indaal.

  • The malting floor. Bowmore is one of the few distilleries that still malts some of its own barley, about 30% of what it uses, turned by hand with wooden shovels. Older guides say the tour lets you have a go, so ask.
  • Warehouse tasting among the casks, a few steps from the sea.
  • The Tasting Bar afterwards. It opens at 11:00, with big windows over the loch and a menu of rarer bottlings you can only try here.
  • Drivers can have their drams in small bottles to take away. Tell them when you book.

Bowmore stopped distilling for the rest of 2026 and hasn't said when it restarts, so the stills may be quiet when we visit. Tours and tastings carry on either way. Spaces are limited, so book early. The visitor centre has stairs, and Bowmore is closed on Sundays.

What visitors say

  • Friendly guides and bar staff who love what they do. Lynn gets several mentions.
  • Generous samples, including drams you can only get at the distillery.
  • The malting floor, the old warehouses and the view over the bay from the bar.

Worth knowing

  • Tours sell out. One visitor couldn't get on one and only saw the shop and bar.
  • A few reviewers found the standard tour less distinctive than others on Islay. If you want more, the Cask Discovery tasting in the No.1 Vaults (£70) is the upgrade.
  • Photos are restricted in the vault.
