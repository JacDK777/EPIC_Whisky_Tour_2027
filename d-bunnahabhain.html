Seanchas · The story

At the mouth of the river

Bunnahabhain was built in 1881 by the Islay Distillery Company, a group that included William Robertson and the Greenlees brothers, and the first spirit ran in 1883. They built a whole village for the workers around it.

For its first eighty years the distillery depended on the sea. Barley, coal and casks came in by boat to its own pier, and the road to Port Askaig was only built in 1960.

Its stills are the tallest on Islay, with long swan necks that make a lighter spirit, and the water comes from a spring by the Margadale River. That's why most Bunnahabhain is unpeated, nutty and sherried. It also goes into the Black Bottle blend.

Don't miss: walk out along the pier after the tasting. The Paps of Jura sit right across the Sound, and it's the best photo of the day.

The distillery

At the end of a long single-track road on the north coast. Bunnahabhain is mostly unpeated, which makes it the gentle surprise of the week.

House style. Nutty, sherried and lightly salty. The island air, without the bonfire.

Ar turas · Our tour

Warehouse 9 Tasting

WhenWed 30 June, 12:30
Lengthabout 1½ hours
Price£50 each
In randabout R1,100

The one reviewers call the highlight. You sit among the casks behind the black No.9 door while a guide draws cask-strength whisky straight from the barrel, both the classic unpeated Bunnahabhain and the smoky Mòine.

  • Four big drams: 25 ml each at cask strength, chosen by the Master Blender.
  • Unpeated and peated side by side. The sherried house style next to Mòine, the smoky Bunnahabhain.
  • Fill your own. Afterwards you can buy a 20 cl bottle hand-filled and labelled from your favourite cask, with £5 off.
  • The view. The distillery looks across its bay to the Paps of Jura, one of the best views on Islay.

18 and over only. Bookings are non-refundable, though they may move you to another time if you ask early. The warehouse is cold, so bring a jacket. There's no café, only whisky at the bar, so bring snacks for the day. Open every day, 10:00 to 17:00.

What visitors say

  • The waterfront setting and the view of the Paps of Jura.
  • Warehouse 9, with samples straight from the cask. One couple called it "one of the most fun distillery tours we have had".
  • Knowledgeable guides and generous pours. Elise, Paul, Kyle, Mark and Cat all get named.
  • The new visitor centre and shop.

Worth knowing

  • The bar only serves whisky: no soft drinks, beer or food.
  • The road in is single track with blind bends. Our driver will be glad of a slow pace.
  • One reviewer found a tour rushed late in the day, so an early-afternoon slot suits us.
