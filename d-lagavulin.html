Seanchas · The story

Below the castle of the Lords of the Isles

Lagavulin Bay was the naval harbour of the MacDonald Lords of the Isles, guarded by Dunyvaig Castle on the rocks at its mouth. The castle was besieged and taken four times in the 1600s and pulled down by the Campbells in 1677.

Smugglers were making whisky in bothies round the bay as early as the 1740s. John Johnston founded the legal distillery in 1816, and by 1837 two neighbouring distilleries had been merged into the one you see today. White Horse owned it by the 1920s, and it is now part of Diageo.

Lagavulin stopped malting its own barley in 1974. The malt now comes from the big Port Ellen maltings, the building you'll walk past on the way home to the house.

Don't miss: walk out on the headland east of the bay to the castle ruins. It's the classic view back across the water to the distillery, and only a few minutes from the visitor centre.

The distillery

Set round a small bay below the ruins of Dunyvaig Castle, once a stronghold of the Lords of the Isles. Lagavulin's slow distillation gives a famously rich, round spirit.

House style. Deep, rounded smoke with dried fruit, sherry sweetness and a long, warming finish.

Ar turas · Our tour

Distillery Exclusive Experience

✓ Included in our R29,000 trip budget
WhenMon 28 June, 10:30
Length2 hours
Price£130 each
In randabout R2,900

Lagavulin's top visit, and a fitting one for a 40th. You see how the spirit is made, then sit down in the Mackie Lounge for a tutored tasting and leave with a bottle you filled yourself.

  • Production tour through the mash house and the still house, with its pear-shaped spirit stills that make Lagavulin so rich.
  • New make spirit. Taste the clear spirit straight off the still, before any cask has touched it.
  • Premium tasting in the Mackie Lounge: four Lagavulin bottlings and a single cask sample.
  • Fill your own bottle. Hand-fill a 20 cl bottle of that single cask whisky to take home.

Up to 12 people per booking, 18+ only, and arrive 15 minutes early. The drams can change with what's available. If you're driving, ask for your drams to take away. Lagavulin is open every day in summer from 10:00 to 17:00.

What visitors say

  • Guides who tell a good story. Many reviewers name theirs and say the stories made the visit.
  • The tastings, with well-paced explanations and a good spread of whiskies.
  • The setting on the bay, with views to Dunyvaig Castle.
  • Filling your own bottle is a favourite.

Worth knowing

  • One reviewer found the cheaper Warehouse No3 Experience scripted for four drams. That's one reason we've gone for the Exclusive.
  • Photos are restricted in parts of the distillery.
  • The tour has stairs and is not suitable for restricted mobility.
  • There's a bar (the Dramming Room, 10:30 to 16:00) but no café, so eat at Ardbeg afterwards.

This is the top experience here, so there are no upgrades to consider.
