Seanchas · The story

The beautiful hollow by the broad bay

Laphroaig was founded in 1815 by two brothers, Alexander and Donald Johnston, who farmed the land here and turned their barley into whisky. The name is usually translated as "the beautiful hollow by the broad bay".

The Johnston family ran it for well over a century. The last of them, Ian Hunter, left the distillery in 1954 to Bessie Williamson, who had come to Islay as a summer secretary. She ran Laphroaig through the 1950s and 60s, one of the very few women ever to run a Scotch distillery.

The story goes that during American Prohibition, Laphroaig's smoky, medicinal flavour helped it through US customs as a medicine rather than a spirit.

Laphroaig is one of the few distilleries that still malts some of its own barley on traditional floors and dries it over a peat fire from its own peat bank. In 1994 the then Prince of Wales gave it a Royal Warrant, and the same year it started the Friends of Laphroaig, the square-foot plots that now include yours.

The distillery

The most outspoken whisky on Islay. Laphroaig still turns some of its barley on its own malting floor and dries it over peat cut from its own moss.

House style. Medicinal smoke, iodine and seaweed over a sweet, vanilla core. You will either love it or remember it.

Ar turas · Our tour

Grain to Glass

WhenSun 27 June, 14:00
Length2.5 hours
Price£90 each
In randabout R2,000

The tour reviewers rate as the best whisky experience on Islay. You follow the whole process, from the barley on the malting floor and the peat fire in the kiln to the stills, then finish in the warehouse tasting straight from the cask.

  • Malting floor and kiln. Laphroaig is one of only a handful of Scottish distilleries that still malts some of its own barley, and you may get to throw a peat onto the fire.
  • Taste the water straight from the spout that feeds the distillery.
  • Cask tasting in the warehouse, with whiskies drawn from the cask that you can't buy in a shop.
  • Small groups and friendly guides. Reviewers keep naming their guides and praising how much they knew.

Book as soon as 2027 slots open on laphroaig.com, because summer tours sell out. If the distillery has a production pause in late June, Laphroaig says the route may change, so check when you book. The non-drinker of the day can ask for drams to take away.

What visitors say

  • Warm, knowledgeable guides and staff who go out of their way, even rebooking guests after a cancelled ferry.
  • The smell of peat smoke, the sea views and the picnic tables on the shore.
  • The warehouse tastings and the chance to try bottlings you can't get at home.

Worth knowing

  • There's a bar but no restaurant, so eat before you go.
  • Grain to Glass can run long with a bigger group.

Caraidean · Friends of Laphroaig

Collect the rent

Every Friend of Laphroaig holds a lifetime lease on one square foot of Islay, in a field beside the distillery. The rent is paid in whisky: show your membership at the visitor centre and they pour you a dram.

Before we go

  1. Everyone else: join free at laphroaig.com/friends. You'll need the code from a bottle of Laphroaig. Do it well before June so the certificates arrive.
