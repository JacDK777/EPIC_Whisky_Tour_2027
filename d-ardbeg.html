Seanchas · The story

The distillery that nearly died

Ardbeg was licensed in 1815 by John MacDougall, though whisky had probably been made here illegally since the 1790s. By 1886 it was the biggest producer on Islay, making 250,000 gallons a year.

The 20th century was hard on it. The company went into liquidation in 1959, the distillery was mothballed in 1981 with every production job lost, restarted in a small way in 1989 and closed again in 1996.

In 1997 Glenmorangie bought it and brought it back to life. By 1998 the old kilns and malting floors had become the café and shop, and Ardbeg has since become one of the cult names in whisky.

Don't miss: the short path to the rocky outcrop by the shore, for the view back to the big ARDBEG letters on the warehouse wall.

An idea for an evening: Ardbeg opened its own boutique hotel, Ardbeg House, in Port Ellen in September 2025. Its restaurant and Islay Bar are open to visitors, with Islay seafood, whisky-smoked barbecue and a deep whisky list. It's in the village, about a 20-minute walk from our house. Book well ahead.

The distillery

The last of the three on the Kildalton coast, with a famous café in the old kiln. Ardbeg is heavily peated yet surprisingly sweet and citrusy underneath.

House style. Huge smoke balanced by lime, espresso, tar and sweet vanilla. Big but never harsh.

Ar turas · Our tour

Classic Tour with Limited Edition Drams

WhenMon 28 June, 13:00
Length1¼ hours
Price£40 each
In randabout R900

The full production tour, from the mash tun to the giant spirit still, with three limited edition drams poured along the way rather than the everyday core range.

  • The mash tun and washbacks: a mash tun from 1961 and six washbacks of Oregon pine. Ardbeg still keeps its records on paper.
  • The purifier. A pipe on the spirit still sends some vapour back down to be distilled again. That gives Ardbeg its fruity, oily spirit under all the smoke. They call it "two and a half times distilled".
  • Limited edition drams tasted at stops along the tour. These are bottlings you rarely see in South Africa.
  • Lunch from the courtyard afterwards, at the summer food trailer by the Old Kiln Café. Reviewers rave about the smoked haddock chowder.

Check this when you book. Ardbeg stops production every summer for its "silent season" (15 June to 12 July in 2026), and the classic tours don't run then. The 2027 dates come out early in 2027. If our Monday falls inside it, book The Roaming Dram at 14:00 instead: same price, four drams. Classic tours are weekdays only; book at least a week ahead.

What visitors say

  • Guides who know their stuff and make it fun. Reviewers name Anna, Emma, Jarred and Diura.
  • Getting right in among the casks in the warehouses.
  • The setting on the rocks by the sea, and a relaxed, friendly feel.
  • Staff in the visitor centre who look after you even if you only drop in.

Worth knowing

  • One reviewer found a guide's humour too crude. It's rare, but it was mentioned.
  • During the silent season the stills are quiet, so you see the kit but not the process.
  • In summer, lunch comes from the food trailer in the courtyard, and it gets busy around 13:00.
  • The visitor centre is open every day from 09:30 to 17:00, May to September.
