Seanchas · The story

The island distillery that came back

The laird, Archibald Campbell, licensed the first distillery here in 1810. It changed hands through the 1800s, and in 1901 the tenants stripped it and walked away.

By the 1950s Jura's population had fallen to around 250. Two landowners, Robin Fletcher and Tony Riley-Smith, rebuilt the distillery to give the island work, with tall stills designed by William Delmé-Evans to make a lighter, more Highland-style whisky. It started distilling again in 1963, doubled to four stills in 1978, and has belonged to Whyte & Mackay since 1994.

George Orwell finished Nineteen Eighty-Four at Barnhill, a remote farmhouse at the north end of the island, in the late 1940s.

Look up: the three Paps of Jura rise right behind Craighouse. Beinn an Òir, the highest, is 785 metres.

The distillery

The only distillery on Jura, an island of 200 people and around 5,000 red deer. George Orwell wrote Nineteen Eighty-Four here. The spirit is light and the stills are among the tallest in Scotland.

House style. Light, sweet and approachable. Toffee, orange and a hint of smoke in some bottlings.

Ma thèid sinn · If we go

Classic Distillery Tour

Optional extra: not in the R29,000 trip budget
WhenThu 1 July, 10:30
Length1 hour
Price£15 each
In randabout R300

A small, informal tour of the only distillery on Jura, in the village of Craighouse at the foot of the Paps. It's as much about the island day out as the whisky.

  • The tall stills. Jura's four stills stand over 25 feet high, among the tallest in Scotland, which is what makes the spirit so light.
  • Small groups. No more than ten people on a tour.
  • Two drams at the end, and a Jura glass to take home.
  • The shop sells bottlings you can only buy at the distillery.

Jura is open Monday to Friday, 10:00 to 16:00, and closed at weekends, so Thursday works. Tours usually run at 10:30 and 14:00. You can cancel or move a booking up to 48 hours before; after that there are no refunds unless the weather stops the ferry. Book early, because there are only a few tours a day.

An t-slighe · Getting there

A day trip to Jura

Jura is just across the Sound from Port Askaig, but Craighouse is on the far side of the island, so it takes a little planning. If we swap the Oa for Jura, the day would look like this:

  1. 08:45 · Taxi to Port Askaig. About 35 minutes from the house. Bus 451 also runs, but isn't always timed to the ferry.
  2. About 09:30 · The Jura ferry to Feolin. A small council ferry, roughly 10 minutes across. No booking: just be at the slipway 5 minutes before it sails, and pay the deckhand.
  3. Feolin to Craighouse. About 8 miles on Jura's single road. Take bus 456 (Garelochhead Coaches) or pre-book a local minibus, such as Spirit of Jura Tours (07736 311 275).
  4. 10:30 · Classic Distillery Tour. One hour.
  5. Lunch at the Jura Hotel, right across the road from the distillery, then a wander along the bay at Craighouse.
  6. Afternoon · back the same way, with dinner at the house.

Check the ferry and bus timetables nearer the time. They change twice a year and the 2027 ones aren't out yet.
